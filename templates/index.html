Safety Protocols

Precautions & SOPs

Full Standard Operating Procedures for all disaster scenarios. Distribute and enforce across all teams.

Flood / Tsunami SOP

  • Issue Level-1 evacuation orders within the first 30 minutes of a flood alert.
  • Deploy NDRF teams to low-lying zones; coordinate with local district authorities.
  • Activate all coastal relief hubs and pre-position boats and life jackets.
  • Broadcast alerts via SENTINEL and all media channels every 15 minutes.
  • Open designated safe zones with minimum 72-hr food and water supplies.
  • Track water levels from IoT sensors and update tactical map in real time.

Cyclone / Hurricane SOP

  • Raise NDMA Cyclone Watch at 72 hrs and Warning at 48 hrs before landfall.
  • Coordinate pre-emptive evacuation of coastal districts — target 100% compliance.
  • Ensure all depots have 7-day supply buffers before cyclone landfall window.
  • Suspend rescue ops during peak wind speed; resume within 2 hrs of eye passage.
  • Post-landfall: deploy rapid damage assessment teams within 4 hrs.
  • Maintain satellite comms backup if terrestrial infrastructure is disrupted.

Earthquake SOP

  • Activate Urban Search & Rescue (USAR) teams within 1 hr of M5.0+ event.
  • Establish incident command at nearest safe structure; set 500m exclusion zone.
  • Deploy K9 and thermal imaging teams for trapped-persons detection.
  • Triage casualties using START method; fast-track critical to field hospitals.
  • Assess structural integrity before re-entry clearance — require engineer sign-off.
  • Monitor aftershock data; suspend ops if M4.0+ aftershock detected.

Wildfire SOP

  • Initiate aerial reconnaissance to map fire perimeter every 2 hrs.
  • Coordinate with Forest Dept for controlled burns to create firebreaks.
  • Pre-position tanker trucks and firefighting aircraft at nearest air base.
  • Evacuate all villages within 5 km of active fire front immediately.
  • Deploy air quality monitoring — issue health advisories for PM2.5 >150 µg/m³.
  • Activate mutual aid agreements with neighboring state fire services.

Field safety protocols for rescue personnel. Follow these at all active incident zones.

Personal Protective Equipment

  • Always wear full PPE before entering any incident zone — helmet, gloves, reflective vest, steel-toed boots.
  • Flood ops: wear life jacket rated for your body weight at all times near water.
  • Fire proximity: use nomex suit, SCBA when smoke is present.
  • Debris fields: use N95 minimum; powered air-purifying respirator for confined spaces.
  • Check PPE integrity before every shift — report damaged equipment immediately.
  • Buddy system mandatory — never enter structure alone.

Communication Protocol

  • Check in with Incident Commander every 30 minutes during active ops.
  • Use radio channel designated by IC — do not switch channels without authorization.
  • MAYDAY procedure: state MAYDAY 3×, unit ID, location, nature of emergency, number of persons.
  • File a Field Report on SENTINEL immediately after any new hazard discovery.
  • Keep radio charged; carry backup satellite phone on extended deployments.
  • Do not use non-explosion-proof devices in areas with gas leak risk.

Casualty Triage (START Method)

  • Immediate (Red): Breathing only after airway repositioning, or >30 breaths/min, no radial pulse.
  • Delayed (Yellow): Breathing, pulse present, not following commands.
  • Minor (Green): Walking wounded — direct to collection point.
  • Expectant (Black): No breathing after airway maneuver — do not expend resources.
  • Re-triage all casualties every 15 min during active rescue phase.
  • Document all casualties — name, tag color, time, injuries — in Field Report.

Hazard Awareness

  • Identify and mark all secondary hazards: gas leaks, live wires, structural instability.
  • Flood water may carry sewage, chemicals, and live electrical current — test before entry.
  • Beware of "dead zones" in collapsed buildings — use listening devices before entering voids.
  • Do not operate heavy equipment within 10m of a confirmed void with live casualties.
  • After-dark ops: mandatory lighting, reduced team size, increase check-in frequency to 15 min.
  • Debrief within 24 hrs of critical incidents — mental health support is mandatory, not optional.

Your personal safety guide. Read and share these with your family before a disaster strikes.

Go-Bag Essentials

Prepare a bag you can grab in 60 seconds.

  • Water — 3 litres per person per day, minimum 3-day supply.
  • Non-perishable food: energy bars, canned goods, dry snacks (3-day supply).
  • Battery-powered or hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts.
  • First aid kit with any personal medications (7-day supply).
  • Torch + extra batteries, whistle, dust masks.
  • Copies of ID, Aadhaar, insurance documents in a waterproof bag.
  • Warm blanket, change of clothes, sturdy closed-toe shoes.
  • Emergency contact list written on paper (not just on your phone).

Family Emergency Plan

  • Agree on two meeting points: one near home, one outside your neighbourhood.
  • Identify your nearest safe zone and two evacuation routes from home.
  • Designate an out-of-area contact everyone will call if local lines fail.
  • Text messages often go through when voice calls cannot — set this expectation.
  • Practice your plan at least once a year — include children and elderly members.
  • Know your neighbours — help each other during an emergency.

During a Flood

  • Move to upper floors immediately — do not wait for official orders if water is rising.
  • Never walk or drive through flood water — 15 cm can knock you off your feet.
  • Turn off electricity at the main breaker before water enters your home.
  • Do not touch electrical switches if you are wet or standing in water.
  • Signal for help from the highest point of your building using bright cloth or torch.
  • After the flood: do not return home until authorities declare it safe.

During a Cyclone

  • Go to a permanent, pucca structure — never shelter in a kutcha house or temporary shed.
  • Stay in the strongest room (usually ground floor, away from windows).
  • Do not go outside when winds appear to stop — this may be the eye of the storm.
  • Unplug all appliances; store valuables in waterproof bags on the highest shelf.
  • Keep your charged phone for emergency calls — avoid unnecessary calls to save battery.
  • After the cyclone: check for gas leaks, downed power lines — report via SENTINEL.
